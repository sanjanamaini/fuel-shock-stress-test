# Fuel Shock Stress Test

**What percentage of trucking companies can survive an oil price shock, and at what scale does survival become likely?**

A bottom-up stress test of road freight profitability across five economies (US, India, China, Germany, Brazil), built in three layers:

1. **Unit economics engine** (Excel): a driver-based per-km cost model for a representative trucking operator in each country: fuel, driver wages, maintenance, tolls, insurance, financing, overhead. Outputs baseline EBIT margin and fuel share of revenue.
2. **Shock and survival engine**: crude oil shock scenarios (magnitude x duration x surcharge pass-through) translated to diesel pump prices through each country's fuel tax wedge, then to margin compression and months-to-cash-breach.
3. **Market layer** (Python): the shock engine applied to the empirical distribution of listed logistics companies' margins and cash buffers, producing the headline number: the share of companies that breach margin or cash thresholds under each scenario, a vulnerability ranking, and a 2022 oil spike backtest identifying the fastest-recovery cohort.

## Why diesel, not crude

The same crude shock produces very different pump price moves per country. Fixed excise duties in Germany and India dampen the percentage change at the pump, while US pump prices track crude nearly one for one. The crude-to-pump beta is a first-class model input, and the cross-country contrast it creates is a core finding of this project.

## Licensing note

Bloomberg, CapitalIQ and PitchBook data are licensed and are never committed to this repository. The public repo contains methodology, code, public-source data and aggregated or derived outputs only. `data/licensed/` is gitignored.
